Case study · Agriculture / IoT

Plant-O-Meter

Improving usability for the app that turns a precision-agriculture device's raw readings into plant health data a farmer can actually use in the field.

Role
Research, wireframes, prototyping
Platform
Mobile companion app
Users
Farmers monitoring plant health
Format
Research-led redesign
Link to Plant-O-Meter page

Background

A useful device, held back by its app

The Plant-O-Meter device collects real plant-health data, but the first version of its companion app made that data hard to use. Feedback and internal review both pointed at the same things: navigation complexity and a general lack of clarity in how information was presented, enough to get in the way of the device's actual value.

Challenge

Turning raw readings into something farmers can act on

The redesign had to make the app itself accessible and genuinely useful for interpreting plant-health data, against a backdrop of other real constraints in the space: equipment reliability, GPS signal consistency, cross-functionality between different GPS systems, and a shortage of precision-equipment technicians to support farmers directly.

Process

Research → personas → wireframes → prototype

  • Research: into farmer preferences and the technology they already use day to day.
  • User personas: built to keep the app experience anchored to real preferences, pain points and expectations rather than assumptions.
  • Wireframes: the new app structure, built directly from research findings.
  • Prototype: interactive, used to test the redesigned interface before committing to it.

My contribution

From pain points to shipped decisions

My role covered understanding user pain points, conducting research, building wireframes against a given direction, and prototyping, then working with development to implement the design. The throughline was simplifying navigation, clarifying information, and making the interface something a farmer would actually want to open in the field.
